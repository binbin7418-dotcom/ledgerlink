## One-liner

Your bank only gives you CSV. Your accounting software wants QIF, OFX or QBO. LedgerLink converts between them — and re-reads its own output to prove every transaction survived.

## The problem

Banks export CSV. Quicken wants QIF or QBO, other tools want OFX, and the formats are just different enough that hand-editing in a spreadsheet eats an afternoon and still drops rows. Online converters want you to upload your full transaction history to a stranger's server.

## What it does

- Drop in any bank or card CSV — LedgerLink detects the delimiter and finds the date, amount, debit/credit, payee, memo, cheque-number and category columns by header synonyms
- Handles the messy real-world cases: `(4.50)` bracket negatives, `1,234.56` thousands separators, comma decimals, split Debit/Credit columns, and a sign-flip switch for credit-card exports
- Writes all three formats in one pass: **QIF**, **OFX 1.02** (SGML), and **QBO** (with the Intuit bank id your software expects)
- **Re-check layer:** each written file is parsed back independently — transaction count, net total, and (for OFX/QBO) FITID uniqueness must match the source, or the report says FAIL
- Honest bookkeeping: exact duplicate rows are removed *and listed*, rows that can't be parsed are rejected *with the reason*, and ambiguous dates like 03/04/2026 are flagged instead of silently guessed
- A `conversion_report.md` documents everything: rows in, converted, duplicates, rejects, totals per format, re-check verdict
- Fully offline. Your transactions never leave your machine

## Honest limits

- Date reading defaults to month-first (US style); a day-first toggle is included, and ambiguous dates are always flagged in the report either way
- StatementSift (our PDF statement converter) is the sibling tool if your bank only gives you PDFs — LedgerLink is for CSV sources
- OFX 2.x (XML) is not written; OFX 1.02 is what current Quicken/desktop tools import
